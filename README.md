## Research question

To what extent do the payroll decisions, sporting results and financial outcomes of MLB franchises reveal distinct maximization behaviours over 2015–2024?

MLB has no hard salary cap: spending is moderated only by the Competitive Balance Tax and revenue sharing, and payrolls range from under $80M to nearly $400M. Sports economics has long framed teams as balancing win maximization against profit maximization. This dissertation uses a revealed-preference approach to infer each franchise's objectives from what it actually does.

## Data and methodology

A panel of all 30 franchises over 10 seasons (300 team-seasons) combining payroll, wins, playoff and World Series outcomes, revenue, operating income, margin, CBT paid and market size (sources: Baseball-Reference, Spotrac, Forbes, MLB).

1. **Econometrics:** OLS and panel models with franchise and season fixed effects linking payroll to win percentage.
2. **Efficiency:** cost per win (payroll ÷ wins).
3. **Typology:** k-means clustering (k = 3) on standardized franchise averages of payroll, win %, operating income, margin and cost per win.
4. **Dynamics:** season-to-season transition matrix and group heatmap per franchise.

## Key findings

- **Payroll matters but explains little on its own.** +$10M of payroll ≈ +0.4 pp of win percentage, with R² = 0.098; the effect holds under fixed effects.
- **Efficiency varies widely.** Average cost per win is $2.08M, from $1.07M (Tampa Bay) to $2.85M (New York Yankees).
- **Three strategic profiles emerge:**

  | Profile | Franchises | Avg payroll ($M) | Win % | Op. margin | Cost per win ($M) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | G1 · Budget discipline and efficiency | 16 | 125.2 | 47.9% | +2.5% | 1.78 |
  | G2 · Balanced optimization under constraint | 11 | 176.7 | 51.1% | +6.3% | 2.33 |
  | G3 · Win maximization | 3 | 235.4 | 56.8% | −15.8% | 2.79 |

- **Market size shapes capacity, not strategy.** Big markets spend more, but groups do not map onto market size.
- **Strategies persist, except heavy spending.** About 78% of G1 and 76% of G2 observations stay in their group from one season to the next, against 20% for G3. Heavy spending is a temporary "window of contention".

Strategic diversity in MLB is structural: the league's institutional framework allows several stable equilibria to coexist rather than pushing franchises toward a single dominant strategy.
